Finance CV

Wall Street format. One page, single column, no distractions. The CV that gets you into the interview room.

The 30-Second Rule

A bank recruiter spends roughly 30 seconds on your CV in the first pass. Most of that time is spent on the top third of the page. Every line must earn its place.

Principle: If a bullet point doesn't demonstrate a hard skill, a quantified result, or a competitive achievement — cut it.

This format is deliberately plain. No colours. No graphics. No creative flourishes. Wall Street CVs signal competence through density, precision, and restraint.

Education Section

For students and recent graduates, education comes first. This is the only time in your career it will.

GPA / Grade

Include your GPA if it's 3.5+ (US) or First Class / high 2:1 (UK/Ireland). If it's lower, leave it out entirely — don't draw attention to it.

Relevant Coursework

Only list courses that are directly relevant to the role. "Introduction to Economics" adds nothing. "Derivatives Pricing" or "Econometrics" shows technical depth.

Don't: List every module you've taken. Pick 4–6 that signal quantitative or financial competence.

Awards & Scholarships

Academic prizes, Dean's List, entrance scholarships — anything competitive goes here. Participation certificates do not.

The Bullet Point Formula

Every bullet under an experience entry should follow this structure:

[Action Verb] + [What You Did — with finance terminology] + [Quantified Result]

Strong Examples

  • Constructed a three-statement financial model for a $2.4B industrial acquisition, identifying 15% cost synergies
  • Analysed 50+ company filings across European TMT sector to support a sell-side equity research initiation report
  • Automated weekly portfolio risk reporting using Python and Bloomberg API, reducing analyst time by 3 hours per week

Action Verbs That Work

Analysed · Built · Conducted · Constructed · Developed · Evaluated · Executed · Forecasted · Identified · Modelled · Presented · Quantified · Restructured · Screened · Synthesised · Valued

Avoid: "Helped with" · "Was responsible for" · "Assisted in" · "Learned about" — these are passive. Own your contributions.

What NOT to Include

  • Photo — Never on a finance CV (US/UK). Automatic rejection at some firms.
  • Colours, graphics, or sidebars — ATS systems can't parse them, and bankers find them unprofessional.
  • "Microsoft Office" — Everyone has it. Say "Advanced Excel (VBA, pivot tables, VLOOKUP)" or don't mention it.
  • Generic hobbies — "Socialising" and "Watching Netflix" tell them nothing. "Marathon running (sub-3:30)" or "Competitive chess (1800 ELO)" shows discipline.
  • Objective / Personal Statement — The WSO format doesn't use one. Your cover letter handles this.
  • References — "Available on request" wastes a line. They'll ask if they want them.

ATS & Formatting Tips

Most banks use Applicant Tracking Systems to filter CVs before a human ever sees them. This template is designed to pass them cleanly.

  • Single column — multi-column layouts break ATS parsing.
  • Standard section headings — "Education", "Experience", "Skills" — don't get creative.
  • No tables, text boxes, or graphics — ATS reads them as blank space.
  • Simple bullet points — standard disc markers only.
  • PDF export — always submit as PDF unless the portal specifically requests .docx.
Tip: Copy-paste your finished CV into a plain text editor. If it reads correctly as raw text, it will pass most ATS systems.
