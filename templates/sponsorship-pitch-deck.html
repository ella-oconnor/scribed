Part 1: Strategy — before you write anything

Audience profile matters most

Your audience profile carries more weight than raw member count. A society with 200 final-year engineering students is worth more to a tech company than one with 2,000 random followers. Lead with who your people are, not how many.

How to structure tiers

Three tiers is the standard. The psychology:

TierPurposeTypical priceWhat to include
Gold / Headline Your dream sponsor. Only 1–2 companies. Premium exclusivity. [2–5x your Silver price] Everything in Silver, plus: headline naming rights, keynote speaking slot, exclusive recruitment access, co-branded events, logo on all materials
Silver / Partner The anchor tier. Where most sponsors land. Best value. [Your base price — this is the "normal" tier] Logo placement, 1–2 named events, speaking opportunity, recruitment promotion, social media features
Bronze / Supporter Low-commitment entry point. Gets them in the door. [40–60% of Silver] Logo on website/materials, one event mention, newsletter inclusion
Pricing rule of thumb

If you have no previous sponsors, start lower than you think — it's easier to raise prices next year than to get zero sponsors this year. For a university society with 500+ members: Bronze €1,000–€2,000 / Silver €3,000–€5,000 / Gold €7,000–€12,000. Scale up or down based on your audience quality and event capacity.

Common mistakes

1. Listing benefits without explaining value — "Logo on website" means nothing. "Your brand in front of 800 students actively pursuing finance careers" means something.
2. Too many tiers — three is perfect. Four confuses people. Two doesn't give enough options.
3. Not including a "custom" line — always say "All tiers can be tailored." This opens the door for sponsors who want something specific.

How to write the intro paragraph

Your intro paragraph should answer three questions in under 50 words: (1) Who are you? (2) Who is your audience? (3) Why should a sponsor care? Lead with the audience, not yourself.
Example — Manchester Entrepreneurship Society

The Manchester Entrepreneurship Society connects sponsor firms with a community of 600+ students pursuing careers in finance, consulting, and technology. Our sponsorship tiers give partner firms meaningful, year-round visibility on campus and a direct line to our most motivated members.

The outreach email

Never send the deck cold. Send a short email (5 sentences max) that prompts them to open it. Follow this structure:

Subject: [Your Society] — Sponsorship for [Year]

Line 1: One sentence saying who you are and your audience size.

Line 2: One sentence about what the sponsor gets (not what you need).

Line 3: One sentence naming previous sponsors or a relevant fact ("Last year, 3 of our members were hired by [Company X]").

Line 4: "I've attached our sponsorship deck — would you have 10 minutes this week for a quick call?"

Line 5: Sign-off with your name and title.

Deals close on calls, not in inboxes

The deck secures the call; the call secures the deal. Avoid trying to close over email — your goal is always to get 10 minutes on the phone or in person. On the call, ask what they want (recruitment, brand awareness, thought leadership) and tailor the tier accordingly.

Negotiation tips

  • If they say "too expensive" — don't drop the price. Drop the tier. "We also have a Silver package at [price] — would that work better for this year?"
  • If they want something custom — say yes. "All tiers can be tailored" is the line that opens the door. Build a bespoke package.
  • If they want to "think about it" — create urgency. "We're closing Gold by [date] and we've had interest from [another firm]."
  • If they say no — ask for next year. "Totally understand. Can I reach out again in [Month] for next year's programme?" Keep the relationship warm.
