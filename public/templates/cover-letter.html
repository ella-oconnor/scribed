Cover letter template

The 4-paragraph structure that hiring managers actually read

How to use this template

The most common cover letter mistake is focusing on yourself instead of on the company. This template corrects that with a four-paragraph structure, each paragraph serving a specific purpose.

  • Read the strategy guide first (5 minutes), then fill in the letter template.
  • Examples use a fictional person called Priya Sharma applying for a graduate marketing analyst role at Monzo. Delete after use.
  • Adapt the letter for every application. Sending the same cover letter to 20 companies is worse than sending none.
  • Takes 20 minutes per application once you've written the first one.

Part 1: Before you write

The one rule

Every sentence in a cover letter should connect your experience to what the company needs. The focus belongs on why you're right for this specific role at this specific company.

The 4-paragraph structure

ParagraphJobLength
1. The hookWhy this company, why this role, why now. Show you've done your research.2-3 sentences
2. Your strongest evidenceThe one experience that proves you can do this job. Be specific.3-4 sentences
3. The second piece of evidenceA different skill or experience that adds to your case. Show range.2-3 sentences
4. The closeWhat you'd bring, enthusiasm for next steps. Confident, not desperate.2 sentences

Research before you write

Before writing a single word, spend 10 minutes answering these about the company and role. If you can't answer them, you haven't done enough research to write a good letter.
  • What does this company actually do? Not from their website — in your own words.
  • What problem does this role solve for them? Read the job description carefully.
  • What have they done recently? A product launch, a funding round, an expansion, a news story.
  • Why do you genuinely want to work here? If the honest answer is "I need a job," find something real or don't apply.

Paragraph-by-paragraph formulas

Paragraph 1: The hook

Formula
I'm applying for [Role] because [specific thing about the company that genuinely interests you]. [One sentence connecting your background to their need.]
Never start with

"I am writing to apply for..." — generic and forgettable. The reader already knows you're applying. Lead with why.

Example — Priya applying to Monzo

I'm applying for the Graduate Marketing Analyst role at Monzo because I've been following how your team turned transparency reports into a genuine brand differentiator — most banks hide their numbers, you publish them. My experience analysing campaign performance at a fintech startup taught me that the best marketing is honest marketing, which is exactly what your team does.

Paragraph 2: Your strongest evidence

Formula
At [Company/University], I [specific thing you did] which [result with numbers if possible]. This taught me [skill that's directly relevant to the role].
Pick the ONE experience that's most relevant. Not three — one. Go deep, not wide. Include a metric if you have one.
Example — Priya

During my placement at a Series A fintech, I owned the weekly performance dashboard for paid acquisition — tracking CAC, conversion rates, and channel attribution across Meta, Google, and TikTok. When I noticed our TikTok CPA was 40% lower than Meta but getting a fraction of the budget, I built the business case to reallocate spend. The result was a 22% reduction in blended CAC over one quarter. That experience gave me a sharp instinct for where money is being wasted in a marketing budget.

Paragraph 3: The second piece of evidence

Formula
Beyond [first experience], I also [different skill or experience]. [Connect it to something in the job description.]
This paragraph shows range. If paragraph 2 was about technical skills, make this one about communication or leadership. If paragraph 2 was analytical, make this one about creativity.
Example — Priya

Beyond performance analytics, I've developed a strong editorial instinct. I ran the content strategy for our university marketing society — growing our LinkedIn following from 200 to 1,800 in one academic year by shifting from promotional posts to opinion pieces written by members. I think Monzo's content team does something similar at a much larger scale, and I'd love to bring that same blend of data and storytelling to the marketing analytics function.

Paragraph 4: The close

Formula
[What you'd bring — one sentence.] [Enthusiasm for next steps — confident, not desperate.]
Never end with

"I would be grateful for the opportunity..." — reads as overly deferential. "Please do not hesitate to contact me..." — reads as formulaic. A brief, confident closing is more effective.

Example — Priya

I'd bring a combination of analytical rigour and creative thinking — someone who can pull insights from a dashboard and turn them into a story your customers actually care about. I'd love the chance to discuss how I could contribute to the team.

Part 4: Before you send — checklist

  • Company name is correct — nothing kills an application faster than the wrong company name
  • Role title matches the job posting exactly
  • Paragraph 1 mentions something specific about the company — not generic praise
  • At least one paragraph includes a number or metric
  • Every paragraph connects your experience to their needs — not just what you did, but why it matters to them
  • No typos — read it out loud, slowly
  • Under one page — if it's longer, cut Paragraph 3
  • The tone is confident, not desperate — no "I would be extremely grateful" or "I believe I could potentially"
  • You've used the same font and formatting as your CV — they should look like a matching set
The 20-minute rule

Your first cover letter takes an hour. Every one after that should take 20 minutes — you rewrite Paragraph 1 (company-specific hook) and adjust Paragraphs 2-3 to emphasise the most relevant experience. The structure stays the same.

Remember

Treat each sentence as an argument for your candidacy. Every sentence should answer the question: "Why should we hire this person?" If a sentence doesn't answer that, cut it.
