General CV — Strategy Guide

The all-rounder for business, operations, public sector, non-profit, and graduate schemes

Not every career path has a rigid CV convention. If you're applying to business roles, graduate schemes, operations, HR, the public sector, or non-profits, you need a CV that's clean, adaptable, and focuses on transferable skills. This template is built for exactly that.

1. One Template, Any Industry — How to Adapt

This CV is designed as a starting point, not a finished product. The structure stays the same, but the emphasis shifts depending on where you're applying.

How to adjust by sector

  • Graduate schemes (e.g. Civil Service Fast Stream, Teach First): Emphasise leadership, teamwork, and societal impact. These schemes value competency-based evidence over technical skills.
  • Operations / Business: Lead with process improvements, efficiency gains, and anything involving data or systems. Use numbers wherever possible.
  • HR / People: Highlight communication, stakeholder management, conflict resolution, and any training or mentoring you've done.
  • Non-profit / Public sector: Focus on mission alignment, volunteering, community engagement, and any work that demonstrates public service motivation.

2. The Experience Bullet Formula

The core skill in CV writing is turning a vague task description into a compelling evidence bullet. Every bullet should follow this structure:

Bullet Formula [Strong verb] + [What you did] + [Measurable result or impact]

Before and after examples

Weak: "Helped with team administration and supported events."

Strong: "Coordinated logistics for 8 society events with 50–200 attendees, managing venue bookings, speaker liaison, and day-of operations."

Weak: "Was involved in the consulting project."

Strong: "Conducted stakeholder interviews with 6 department heads and synthesised findings into a process improvement report, identifying £40K in annual cost savings."

Weak: "Assisted with social media."

Strong: "Managed the organisation's LinkedIn presence, growing followers by 25% over 4 months through a weekly content calendar and targeted engagement strategy."

Power verbs to use

  • Leadership: Led, Directed, Oversaw, Chaired, Founded, Spearheaded
  • Analysis: Analysed, Evaluated, Assessed, Researched, Investigated, Mapped
  • Communication: Presented, Drafted, Negotiated, Facilitated, Briefed, Liaised
  • Execution: Delivered, Implemented, Coordinated, Managed, Organised, Streamlined

3. Making Non-Glamorous Experience Sound Strong

Not everyone has a brand-name internship. That's fine — recruiters in these sectors know that. What matters is how you describe what you did, not where you did it.

Retail, hospitality, and part-time work

These roles build genuine commercial skills. Reframe them in business language:

  • "Worked the till" → "Processed 100+ customer transactions per shift, maintaining accuracy under time pressure"
  • "Helped customers" → "Resolved customer queries and complaints, consistently receiving positive feedback and maintaining a 4.8/5 satisfaction score"
  • "Trained new people" → "Onboarded and mentored 4 new team members across the first two weeks of employment"
Common Mistake Don't hide or omit non-corporate roles. An empty CV with one internship looks worse than a CV showing consistent work experience across different settings. Recruiters value work ethic.

University projects and coursework

If the project was substantial (group project, dissertation, case study competition), treat it like a professional entry:

"Led a 5-person team in a simulated business strategy exercise, delivering a market entry proposal for a real SME client. Received the highest mark in the cohort (82%)."

4. Extracurriculars Matter — More Than You Think

For graduate-level applications, your Extracurriculars & Leadership section can be just as impactful as your Experience section. This is where you show initiative, responsibility, and character.

How to describe society roles

Don't just list the title — describe what you actually did and the scale of your impact:

"As President of the Entrepreneurship Society (180 members), secured £3,000 in sponsorship, organised a 6-event speaker series, and grew membership by 40% year-on-year."

How to describe volunteering

Volunteering demonstrates values and initiative. Describe it with the same rigour as paid work:

"Volunteered weekly at Leeds North Food Bank, sorting and distributing food parcels for 60+ families. Trained 3 new volunteers on stock management procedures."
Inclusion Threshold If you cannot describe the role in bullet-point format with at least one specific outcome, it is probably not worth including. One well-described role is stronger than three vague ones.

5. Section Order for General Applications

This template uses the following order, optimised for graduate-level generalist applications:

  1. Name & Contact — clean, professional, complete
  2. Education — degree, grade, relevant modules; comes first because it's your strongest credential at graduate level
  3. Experience — internships, part-time roles, freelance work, in reverse chronological order
  4. Extracurriculars & Leadership — society roles, volunteering, competitions, personal projects
  5. Skills — technical tools, languages, certifications; grouped and concise

Once you have 2+ years of post-graduation experience, move Experience above Education. Until then, lead with your degree.

No Profile Summary Unlike creative roles, a general CV doesn't need a profile summary. Recruiters in business, operations, and public sector roles prefer to see evidence immediately. If you want to add context about your interests or career direction, do it in your cover letter.
